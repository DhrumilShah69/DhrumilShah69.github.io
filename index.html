01 · LIVE SINCE 1 SEP 2026

A sales CRM where no lead can be left without a next step

Next.jsSupabase / PostgresRow-level security56 migrations

Problem

Every sales enquiry lived in one shared Excel file. It was hard for anyone to keep up to date, and in one long sheet it was easy to miss a lead, even a high-priority one. Nobody could quickly see how many enquiries had come in, who was following up on what, or which leads had gone quiet.

Users

  • A 6-person sales team who cold-call all day and need to log a call in seconds.
  • The CEO, who wants one screen of what's moving and what's stuck.
  • Execution and design teams, who receive won jobs.

Key decisions

DecisionWhyTrade-off
Build, not buyAn off-the-shelf CRM (about ₹1,600/month for 4 users) covered the basics. We built because tender and bid tracking, our differentiator, isn't sold anywhere, and we wanted the rules below.We own maintenance. I documented every decision in the repo so it isn't one person's memory.
"Every open lead has a next action and a date"The core failure was leads going quiet. I made it a database rule, not a reminder, so a lead cannot be saved without one.Slightly more typing per save. Result: 46 of 46 open leads have a next step.
Enquiry vs leadMost cold calls never become real opportunities. Keeping them as light "enquiries" until qualified keeps the pipeline honest.Two lists to explain; solved with one "Today" screen that merges what's due.
Everyone sees everythingA 6-person team loses more to hidden work than to oversharing. "Owner" means who chases it, not who may look.Revisit if the team grows past ~15.
Host on the company's existing planThe free tier of the first host doesn't allow commercial use. Moved to the web plan the company already paid for, on its own subdomain.Slower builds; documented the workaround.
Remove a feature nobody usedTook out a separate "Focus" planning screen to keep the app to a few screens the team uses daily; kept the code to restore later.Less to show, less to learn.

02 · LIVE SINCE 1 AUG 2026

One HR & admin portal for claims, leave and daily work reports

Next.jsGoogle Sheets + Drive as the databaseVercel37 pull requests

Problem

HR and admin work was scattered and mostly on paper. Every reimbursement meant a paper bill and a form carried from desk to desk for CXO signatures: slow for staff, and a pile of paperwork for accounts.

Once claims moved online, the same problem showed up everywhere else. Employees couldn't see how many leaves they had left, so they couldn't plan ahead. Management had no view of who was working on what each day. And every tender meant collecting its files in one place and chasing two approvals by hand.

Users

  • 25 staff: office, sales and site engineers, often on a phone at a site.
  • Approvers: accounts, the COO and the CEO.
  • HR, who needs leave balances and policies in one place.

What shipped, in order

  • Reimbursements (Aug): submit with bill → accounts check → COO approves → CEO also approves above ₹25,000; daily email to each approver; "paid" status from accounts.
  • Leave (Aug): balances, holidays, manager → HR approval; leave over 2 days also goes to the CEO.
  • Daily work report (Sep): each person lists today's tasks and ticks them off; managers assign tasks; an 11 pm PDF tells leadership who didn't submit.
  • Policies, overtime, tenders, site visit logs (Sep).

Key decisions

DecisionWhyTrade-off
Google Sheets as the databaseAccounts already lived in Sheets and wanted to see and fix rows themselves. The brief said Postgres; the users said Sheets.Google's rate limit is the ceiling. I measured requests per screen and cut them from 26 to 15 across 8 screens.
Bills are private by defaultBills and leave documents carry personal data. Since Sep, files are shared with nobody and open only through the portal after a permission check; 72 old open links were removed.Approvers can't forward a Drive link; that's the point.
Security pass before rollout to all staffSix fixes: access checks so one user can't open another's claim, input sanitising, login rate limit, audit log, safe redirects, 20-minute idle logout.One week with no new features.
Fix the bug, not the symptomWhen two people saved their work report at the same moment, one overwrote the other. Found the root cause in how rows were appended and fixed it in every tab, not just the one reported.A larger change, but the bug can't come back elsewhere.
Upload big files in 3 MB piecesThe host rejects requests over ~4.5 MB, and site engineers photograph bills on phones. Chunked uploads allow 25 MB PDFs.More code paths to test.

Results

79claims from 15 people; 51 approved, 8 sent back for missing info
30 hmedian from applying for leave to a decision (19 requests)
876work-report tasks from 20 people in 29 days; 64% marked done
18people filed a work report on the busiest day (of 25)
1,187actions in the audit log

Learned / next

I didn't record how long claims took before the portal, so I can't show a before/after. Next time I'll take a baseline before launch. Next up: phone clock-in tied to project sites, and one "My approvals" page across claims, leave and overtime.

03 · PROTOTYPE · LLM EVALUATION PRACTICE

An AI website assistant, measured before it's trusted

Gemini APIPythonLangfuse tracesLLM-as-judge

Problem

Website visitors ask the same questions (price ranges, subsidy, products) and many leave without contacting sales. A chat assistant could answer and capture the enquiry, but only if its answers are correct and grounded in what the company actually offers.

What I built

  • A Q&A bot that answers only from the company's website text, sent with every question (grounding; no fine-tuning).
  • Tracing for every reply: latency, tokens, model, errors and fallbacks, viewable in Langfuse.
  • A 27-question test set, a second model as judge (correct, grounded, followed instructions), and a compare command to check a new prompt against the old one.

Results so far

76%of judged answers passed (16 of 21)
2.5 smedian reply; 5 s at p95
~3,900tokens in per question, ~60 out

The failures were useful: the bot left out numbers it had (a discount range, a product's price band) and missed a product for a "factory parking" question. A free-tier quota day pushed the fallback rate to 55%, so the judge now stops instead of retrying.

Next

Hand-mark 10 answers to measure how often the AI judge agrees with a human; write prompt v2 for the failure cases and compare v1 vs v2. Only then pitch it for the live site.

04 · DISCOVERY · NOT BUILT YET

A projects portal for design, purchase and site teams

DiscoveryCompetitor researchClickable prototype

Problem

Project tracking lived in a detailed spreadsheet with a full stage plan, but its task statuses were almost never updated. The plan wasn't the problem; updating it from a site on a phone was.

What I did

  • Mapped the hand-offs: drawings approved for construction (design → purchase) and material at site (purchase → execution).
  • Kept the spreadsheet's good ideas (standard task list, auto-planned dates, health colours) and removed the copy-paste steps that stopped people using it.
  • Researched an established HR and projects suite to decide what to copy (site-tagged attendance, budget vs actual) and what not to (hourly billing, payroll).
  • Built a clickable prototype with example data: dashboard, "My tasks" per team, daily site update, task template, plan check against the target date.

Success will mean

Every active project updated at least daily from site, and leadership running the morning call from the dashboard instead of phone calls.

How I work

  1. Start from the failure, not the feature. "Leads go quiet" became a database rule; "nobody updates the sheet" became a phone-first task list.
  2. Mock it before building it. Clickable prototypes with example data get feedback in a day.
  3. Ship small and often. 475 commits and 37 reviewed pull requests across the two live apps in 11 weeks.
  4. Protect people's data by default. Private files, access checks, audit logs before rollout.
  5. Measure. Usage from the data itself, request counts per screen, and for AI: test sets, judges and traces.
